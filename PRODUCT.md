# PRODUCT.md — Emanuel Tickets

register: product

## Product purpose

Events and ticketing for Temple Emanuel. Congregants discover upcoming events and RSVP or buy tickets in a few taps (no account needed). Temple staff ("hosts") sign in with their Microsoft accounts to create and manage events, set ticket types and capacity, and see who's coming. The public event page is the shareable front door; the host side is a focused management tool.

## Users

- **Attendees / congregants** — all ages, mostly on phones, non-technical. They arrive from a shared link or the events home, deciding whether to attend. They check out as guests (email only), so legibility, trust, and a frictionless RSVP/purchase matter more than power features. Assume some older users: generous type, high contrast, obvious tap targets.
- **Hosts** — temple staff, clergy, and admins. Sign in with Microsoft (Azure Entra, single-tenant). Mostly at a desktop, setting up events ahead of time and checking attendance. They value clarity and speed over density; they are not power analysts.

## Brand

Temple Emanuel heritage, expressed through a ported design system: **Deep Sapphire** (institutional trust, primary actions), **Warm Gold** (heritage, celebration, used sparingly), warm-stone neutrals (a calm, papery canvas, never stark white/black). The **Rose Window** mark (RoseSpinner) is the signature — a nod to the sanctuary's stained glass — reserved for brand moments and loading, not general UI color. The feeling: warm, welcoming, trustworthy, unhurried. A house of community, not a tech product.

## Tone

Warm and plainspoken. Invitational ("Come, let us gather"). Confident but never salesy or urgent. No countdown-timer pressure, no marketing hype, no cutesy kitsch. Copy respects the reader's time and intelligence.

## Anti-references

- **Partiful** — too playful/loud/party-coded for an institution serving all generations.
- **Eventbrite** — cluttered, ad-heavy, upsell-driven, generic.
- **Generic SaaS dashboards** — hero-metric templates, cold gradients, dark "techy" chrome.
- Anything that feels like a startup growth funnel rather than a community gathering.

## Strategic principles

- **Clarity over cleverness.** If a congregant's parent can't use it on a phone, it's wrong.
- **The event page is a front door**, not a form. It should make someone want to come.
- **Mobile-first for attendees, desktop-comfortable for hosts.**
- **Trust at the moment of payment** — clear price, clear what they get, no surprises.
- **Restraint.** Gold and the Rose mark are seasoning, not the meal.
